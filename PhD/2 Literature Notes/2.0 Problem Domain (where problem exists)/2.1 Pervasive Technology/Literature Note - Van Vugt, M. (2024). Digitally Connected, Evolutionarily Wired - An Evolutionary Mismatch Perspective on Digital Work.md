Work habits and preferences could also make a difference in experiencing mismatch.  People who find it difficult to integrate work and private life—so-called segmenters—may experience greater mismatch problems of all kinds, and thus distress, when working digitally remotely than workers who find it easy to quickly switch between work life and private life—so-called integrators (Becker & Lanzl, 2023; Kniffin et al., 2021). Similarly, workers with lower self-control could experience more (physical) mismatch in the digital context, resulting in a lack of physical movement, overeating, and obesity as a consequence. Finally, having a higher IQ–or better executive functioning–may help individuals deal with the evolutionary novelty of the digital workplace, and shield them from experiencing stress and decreased psychological well-being in a mismatched environment (Kanazawa & Li, 2018)
